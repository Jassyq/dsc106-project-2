Does the Previous Year's Savings Boost a Country's GDP Growth

FOR the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Used the color green to indicate growth
  • Skipped a few points to show overall positve trend
  • Divided it into 10 percent bins to show growth

AGAINST the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Used the color red to indicate decline
  • made super large bins to remove noise
  • Plotted very few points to show a sharp decline

Final Reflection

I analyzed the World Bank's economy and growth dataset to explore the relationship between a country's savings ratio and its GDP. My initial approach compared current-year savings ratios with current-year GDP, but this showed no meaningful correlation. Realizing that the economic impact of savings may not show until much later, I revised my method to compare the previous year’s savings ratio with the current year’s GDP. To better visualize trends, I experimented with bin sizes. Larger bins helped smooth out short-term fluctuations and revealed a surprising overall trend: higher savings ratios often correlated with lower GDP growth in the following year. I used red in the visualization to highlight this negative relationship, drawing on its association with market downturns. To explore the opposite perspective, I used smaller bins and selectively removed outlier data points that showed a decline. This revealed segments where higher savings ratios were associated with rising GDP. I visualized this trend in green, reflecting its typical association with economic growth.
